Counterspace, 1957–2026: the weapons and the law that trailed them

Destructive anti-satellite testing has paused since November 2021. Jamming, spoofing, dazzling and cyber operations have not. SWF’s 2026 assessment finds that “only non-destructive capabilities are actively being used against satellites in current military operations” (Executive Summary, p. xxiii). This page puts the technology and the law on a single year axis.

Low Earth orbit (to ~2,000 km) holds imaging satellites, the ISS and broadband constellations. GPS sits in medium Earth orbit (~20,200 km). Broadcast and communications satellites sit in the geostationary belt (~35,786 km). Every destructive intercept so far has been in LEO.

          Co-orbital proximity operations (RPO)

          Rendezvous, docking, capture, release and spaceplane-mission rows from SWF’s co-orbital tables, one lane per actor. Each mark sits at the start date of a ledger row; shape shows the activity and colour the actor.

          A proximity operation is not an attack. Most rows are inspection, servicing or technology demonstrations, and SWF’s wording on intent is hedged (“possibly”, “appeared to”, “likely”). The ledger records that an operation happened, not why; outlined marks show where SWF itself hedges.

            B · Capability diffusion

            Number of states holding each counterspace capability, by decade, on a zero baseline. Solid bands are demonstrated; hatched are developing or latent; faded decades are reconstructed, not SWF-assessed.

            About the coding

            The number above each decade is a range: demonstrated only, to demonstrated plus developing (the whisker). In the 2020s the states are SWF-assessed, but the demonstrated/developing split is the builder’s coding of SWF text, so that range is the uncertain part. Dotted bands are developing entries where SWF’s country matrix shows no data. Decades before 2020 are reconstructed from SWF’s test tables and country chapters. See the coding rules.
