Counterspace, 1957–2026: the weapons and the law that trailed them

Destructive anti-satellite testing has paused since November 2021. Jamming, spoofing, dazzling and cyber operations have not. SWF’s 2026 assessment finds that “only non-destructive capabilities are actively being used against satellites in current military operations” (Executive Summary, p. xxiii). This page puts the technology and the law on a single year axis.

Low Earth orbit (to ~2,000 km) holds imaging satellites, the ISS and broadband constellations. GPS sits in medium Earth orbit (~20,200 km). Broadcast and communications satellites sit in the geostationary belt (~35,786 km). Every destructive intercept so far has been in LEO.

          Satellites that fly close to other satellites

          Almost every operation in the records begins after 2005, and all three states have operations that are still going.

          A proximity operation is not an attack. Most are inspection, servicing or technology demonstrations, and the Secure World Foundation (SWF) words its statements about intent with hedges such as “possibly”, “appeared to” and “likely”. The records show that an operation took place; they do not show why. Outlined points show where SWF itself hedges.

            Which states hold which counterspace capabilities, by decade

            Each bar counts the capabilities states hold in one decade; only the 2020s bar rests on the Secure World Foundation’s (SWF) own assessment, and the earlier decades are reconstructed.

              How we classified these

              For the 2020s, the states and capabilities come from SWF’s 2026 assessment of 13 countries. Whether a capability counts as demonstrated (tested or used) or as still developing is our reading of SWF’s text, so the range bar is the uncertain part: its low end counts demonstrated capabilities only, and its high end adds those still developing. Dotted segments are capabilities we class as developing where SWF’s country table has no data. Decades before 2020 are reconstructed from SWF’s test tables and country chapters; SWF did not assess them.

              Direct-ascent anti-satellite weapons are missiles launched from Earth to hit a satellite. Co-orbital means a satellite that manoeuvres close to another. Electronic warfare means jamming and spoofing. Directed energy means lasers and similar beams. Cyber means attacks through computers and networks. The full rules are in Sources and method.
